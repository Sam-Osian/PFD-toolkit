About PFD Toolkit

Turning Prevention of Future Death reports into usable evidence.

PFD Toolkit makes Prevention of Future Death reports searchable, filterable, and analysable with AI workflows. It is designed for policy, safety, and research teams that need transparent, repeatable analysis at national scale.

Purpose

What problem this solves

Prevention of Future Death (PFD) reports are among the most important public safety signals in the UK, but they are difficult to analyse consistently at scale. Reports are typically published as individual documents, metadata quality can vary, and manual review across many years is resource-intensive.

PFD Toolkit reduces this friction by combining archive-wide search and filtering with structured AI workflows. The aim is to help teams identify relevant reports faster, compare patterns more consistently, and produce outputs that can be reviewed and shared.

Coverage

What the platform contains

  • Report archive: Prevention of Future Death reports from the published source archive.
  • Search and filters: query by text plus metadata dimensions such as date range, coroner area, and receiver.
  • Investigation workflows: stage-based runs for screening/filtering and structured extraction.
  • Exportable outputs: tabular results that can be reviewed, validated, and used downstream.

Feature availability may change as workflows are improved and validated over time.

Workflow

How investigations work

  1. Define the question: provide a clear investigation title and query description.
  2. Set scope: run across the full archive or a constrained date window.
  3. Choose methods: run screening/filtering and, where needed, structured extraction.
  4. Review outputs: inspect run status, metadata, and resulting files in workspace views.

Runs can be executed via the default managed route or via user-provided OpenAI configuration for advanced workflows.

AI methods

How AI is used in practice

PFD Toolkit uses a type of AI model called a Large Language Model (LLM). An LLM is a model trained on large amounts of text so it can recognise patterns in language and produce structured outputs from written material.

In practical terms, the model reads report text and helps with tasks such as deciding whether a report matches your investigation criteria and extracting requested fields into table form. It is useful for speed and consistency, but it is not a source of truth.

All outputs should be reviewed by users and interpreted in context. The system is designed to support expert judgement, not replace it.

Evidence

Validation and quality

PFD Toolkit has been benchmarked in research settings against expert review. Current published evaluation reports strong agreement and faster end-to-end processing compared with manual-only approaches.

  • Agreement benchmark: 97% agreement with expert clinician judgements.
  • Comparative yield: 73 identified cases using PFD Toolkit versus 37 identified manually by a research team in the benchmark comparison.
  • Runtime: 5:29 end-to-end in the published validation example.

Reference: BMJ Mental Health, doi: 10.1136/bmjment-2025-302212.

Responsible use

Limits and interpretation

  • AI outputs can contain errors and should be reviewed before policy or operational decisions.
  • Results are sensitive to query wording, selected scope, and extraction specification.
  • Absence of a match does not imply absence of risk.
  • The platform is designed to support analysis and review, not provide legal or clinical advice.
